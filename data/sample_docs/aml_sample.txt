Anti-Money Laundering — Customer Due Diligence

A reporting entity must establish the identity of every client before opening an
account. Identity is established by referring to an original, valid government-issued
photo document and one secondary document showing the client's current address.

Where the client is a corporation, the entity must obtain the names of all directors
and of every person who owns or controls 25 percent or more of the corporation, and
must confirm the existence of the corporation from a public registry record.

Ongoing monitoring applies for the life of the relationship. Transactions that are
inconsistent with the client's known profile must be reviewed, and the review and its
outcome must be recorded. Records of identity verification must be kept for at least
five years after the account is closed.
